Orthodox saints and name days on August 30

The Orthodox Church commemorates 7 saints and feasts on August 30. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on September 12 of the civil calendar.

Saints and feasts of August 30

  • Alexander the Patrician

    Martyr, Αλέξανδρος

    St. Alexander was a martyr of the early Church who confessed Christ before his persecutors and sealed his faith with his blood. The synaxaria preserve little of his life beyond his noble standing and his faithful confession unto death.

    Name days: Alexandros, Alekos, Alex, Alessandro, Alexander, Alexandra, Aleka

  • John and Paul the New, Patriarchs of Constantinople

    Hierarch, Ιωάννης και Παύλος ο Νέος

    Saint John the Faster served as Patriarch of Constantinople in the sixth century and was renowned for his fasting and almsgiving. Saint Paul the New held the same throne in the eighth century and resigned it in penitence for having served under the iconoclasts.

  • Alexander Nevsky

    Saint, Αλέξανδρος Νιέφσκι

    Saint Alexander was Prince of Novgorod and later Grand Prince of Vladimir in the thirteenth century. He defended Orthodox Rus against the Swedes and the Teutonic Knights while dealing wisely with the Mongols, and before his death he received the monastic schema.

  • Alexander of Svir

    Venerable, Αλέξανδρος του Σβερ

    Saint Alexander was a monk of Valaam who withdrew to the wilderness by the river Svir, where a monastery grew up around him. Tradition relates that he was granted a vision of the Holy Trinity.

  • Phantinos the Wonderworker

    Venerable, Φαντίνος

    Saint Phantinos was a Greek monk of Calabria in southern Italy who lived in great asceticism and later travelled to Thessaloniki, where he reposed in the tenth century.

  • Vryaine the Ascetic

    Holy Woman, Βρυαίνη

    Saint Vryaine was an ascetic woman who lived in prayer and stillness and reposed in peace.

  • Philonides of Kourion

    Hierarch, Φιλωνίδης

    Saint Philonides was bishop of Kourion in Cyprus during the persecution of Diocletian. Tradition relates that he gave up his soul to God rather than be defiled by his persecutors.
