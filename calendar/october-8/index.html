Orthodox saints and name days on October 8

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on October 8. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on October 21 of the civil calendar.

Name days on October 8

Pelagia, Pelayia, Taisia, Thais, Taisiya

Saints and feasts of October 8

  • Pelagia

    Holy Woman, Πελαγία

    St. Pelagia was a celebrated actress of Antioch, renowned for her beauty and wealth, who was brought to compunction by the preaching of a holy bishop. She was baptized, gave her riches to the poor, and departed secretly to Jerusalem, where she lived in a cell on the Mount of Olives in strict asceticism, unknown to all until her repose.

    Name days: Pelagia, Pelayia

  • Pelagia the Virgin of Antioch

    Holy Woman, Πελαγία η Παρθένος

    Pelagia was a fifteen-year-old Christian of Antioch during the persecution under Numerian. When soldiers came to seize her, she asked leave to change her garments and, praying, threw herself from the roof of the house to preserve her purity.

  • Thais of Egypt

    Holy Woman, Ταϊσία

    Thais was a woman of Alexandria who lived a life of sin until the abba Paphnutios came to her disguised as a customer and spoke to her of the judgment of God. Struck with compunction, she burned her possessions in the public square.

    Name days: Taisia, Thais, Taisiya

  • Ignatios the New Martyr of Bulgaria

    Martyr, Ιγνάτιος ο Οσιομάρτυς

    Born John in Eski Zagora of Bulgaria, he was raised in piety but in a moment of weakness he had once professed Islam under pressure. Repenting bitterly, he went to Mount Athos and became a monk named Ignatios under the elders of the Skete of the Forerunner.

  • Tryphon of Vyatka

    Venerable, Τρύφων της Βιάτκα

    Tryphon was born in the mid-sixteenth century in the Mezen region and became a monk in Perm, where he preached to the pagan Ostyaks and Voguls. He later founded the Dormition monastery at Vyatka and served as its archimandrite.

  • Philip of Gortyna

    Hierarch, Φίλιππος επίσκοπος Γορτύνης

    Philip was Bishop of Gortyna, the ancient capital of Crete, in the second century and is praised by Saint Dionysios of Corinth for the steadfastness of his church.
