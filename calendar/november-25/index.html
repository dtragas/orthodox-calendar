Orthodox saints and name days on November 25

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on November 25. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on December 8 of the civil calendar.

Saints and feasts of November 25

  • Catherine the Great Martyr

    Martyr, Αικατερίνη η Μεγαλομάρτυς

    St. Catherine the Great Martyr was a noble and brilliantly educated maiden of Alexandria, martyred at the beginning of the fourth century. According to tradition, she confessed Christ before the emperor and bested in debate the philosophers gathered to refute her, leading many to the faith. After torture on the wheel, which was broken by grace, she was beheaded.

    Name days: Aikaterini, Katerina, Katia, Katina, Kate, Cathy, Cat, Katherine, Kathryn, Catherine, Ekaterini, Kaiti, Katie, Kathy

  • Mercurius the Great Martyr

    Martyr, Μερκούριος ο Μεγαλομάρτυς

    Saint Mercurius was a Scythian officer in the Roman army who, according to tradition, was granted victory in battle by an angel and afterwards confessed Christ before the Emperor Decius. He was subjected to cruel tortures and beheaded at Caesarea in Cappadocia.

    Name days: Merkourios, Mercurius, Mercurios, Merkouris

  • Peter the Hesychast

    Venerable, Πέτρος ο Ησυχαστής

    Venerable Peter was born in Galatia and, according to Theodoret of Cyrrhus, left home at the age of seven to live as an ascetic. He settled in a tomb near Antioch where he lived for many years in silence and prayer.

  • Augusta the Empress

    Holy Woman, Βασίλισσα Αυγούστα

    Saint Augusta was the wife of the emperor Maxentius. Tradition relates that she visited Saint Catherine in prison, was converted by her and confessed Christ before her husband.

  • Porphyrios the General

    Martyr, Πορφύριος ο Στρατηλάτης

    Saint Porphyrios was a general of the emperor Maxentius who, together with two hundred soldiers, believed in Christ through the witness of Saint Catherine and the Empress Augusta.

  • The 150 Rhetors

    Saint, Οι Εκατόν Πενήντα Ρήτορες

    Tradition relates that the emperor Maxentius summoned the most learned philosophers of the empire to dispute with Saint Catherine. Defeated by her wisdom, they confessed Christ.
