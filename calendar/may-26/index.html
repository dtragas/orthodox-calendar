Orthodox saints and name days on May 26

The Orthodox Church commemorates 7 saints and feasts on May 26. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on June 8 of the civil calendar.

Name days on May 26

Saints and feasts of May 26

  • Karpos the Apostle

    Apostle, Κάρπος

    The Holy Apostle Karpos was one of the Seventy, a companion and fellow laborer of St. Paul. The Apostle mentions him in his second letter to Timothy, recalling the cloak and books he had left with Karpos at Troas. Tradition holds that he served the Church as a bishop and gave himself wholly to the preaching of the Gospel.

    Name days: Karpos

  • Alphaeus the Apostle

    Apostle, Αλφαίος ο Απόστολος

    Alphaeus is named in the Gospels as the father of the Apostle James the Less and, according to tradition, of the Evangelist Matthew. Tradition relates that he preached the Gospel and reposed in peace.

    Name days: Alfaios, Alphaeus

  • Alexander the Dervish

    Martyr, Αλέξανδρος ο Δερβίσης

    Alexander was born in Thessalonica and as a young man embraced Islam, becoming a wandering dervish. Repenting, he returned to Christ and travelled to Smyrna, where he openly declared his faith before the judge.

  • Synesios of Karpasia

    Hierarch, Συνέσιος Καρπασίας

    Synesios was bishop of Karpasia in the north-east of Cyprus, honored on the island as a wonderworker. Tradition places him in the early Christian centuries; his church at Rizokarpaso preserves his memory.

    Name days: Synesios, Synesius

  • Averkios the Martyr

    Martyr, Αβέρκιος

    Tradition relates that Averkios and his sister Helen were children of the Apostle Alphaeus. Averkios was tied naked among bees and died of their stings for confessing Christ.

  • Helen the Martyr

    Holy Woman, Ελένη η Μάρτυς

    Helen, sister of the martyr Averkios and, according to tradition, daughter of the Apostle Alphaeus, was stoned to death for confessing Christ.

  • George the New Martyr of Bulgaria

    Martyr, Γεώργιος ο Νεομάρτυς εκ Βουλγαρίας

    George was a young Bulgarian from Sofia, a goldsmith by trade, who was pressed by the Turks to embrace Islam. Refusing, he confessed Christ and was burned alive.
