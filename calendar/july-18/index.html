Orthodox saints and name days on July 18

The Orthodox Church commemorates 7 saints and feasts on July 18. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on July 31 of the civil calendar.

18July

Name days on July 18

Aimilanos, Emilianos, Emilios, Valentina, Valentini, Oualentini, Valentine

Saints and feasts of July 18

  • Emilian of Dorostolum

    Martyr, Αιμιλιανός

    St. Emilian was a Christian of Dorostolon on the Danube who suffered martyrdom in the fourth century, in the reign of Julian the Apostate. Moved by zeal for the true God, he entered a pagan temple and overturned the idols; when another man was blamed for the deed, he came forward and confessed it freely rather than let the innocent suffer.

    Name days: Aimilanos, Emilianos, Emilios

  • Paul, Valentina and Thea

    Martyr, Παύλος, Ουαλεντίνη και Θέη

    St. Paul and the sisters Valentina and Thea were Egyptian Christians brought to Caesarea in Palestine during the persecution of Maximian in the early fourth century. When Thea was tortured, her sister Valentina overturned the pagan altar and was condemned with her.

    Name days: Valentina, Valentini, Oualentini, Valentine

  • Pambo of Egypt

    Venerable, Παμβώ

    St. Pambo was one of the great desert fathers of Nitria in Egypt in the fourth century, a disciple of St. Anthony the Great. Unlettered at first, he learned the Psalms and was known for his silence, his humility and his labor with his hands.

  • Hyakinthos of Amastris

    Martyr, Υάκινθος ο εξ Αμάστριδος

    St. Hyakinthos was a Christian of Amastris on the Black Sea who cut down a tree venerated by the pagans. For this he was tortured and put to death.

  • Markellos the Martyr

    Martyr, Μάρκελλος

    St. Markellos is commemorated as a martyr who confessed Christ during the persecutions. Tradition relates that he died by fire.

  • Dasios and Maron

    Martyr, Δάσιος και Μάρων

    Sts. Dasios and Maron are commemorated together as martyrs who confessed Christ during the persecutions of the early Church. Tradition relates that they died by the sword.

  • John the Long-Suffering of Kiev

    Venerable, Ιωάννης ο Πολύτλας

    St. John the Long-Suffering was a monk of the Kiev Caves in the twelfth century who struggled for thirty years against the passions. Tradition relates that he buried himself up to the chest in the earth of his cave and remained there in prayer.
