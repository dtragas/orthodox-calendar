Orthodox saints and name days on January 30

The Orthodox Church commemorates 8 saints and feasts on January 30. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on February 12 of the civil calendar.

Name days on January 30

Saints and feasts of January 30

  • Hippolytos

    Martyr, Ιππόλυτος

    St. Hippolytos was a martyr of the Church of Rome who gave his life for Christ during the persecutions of the third century. The synaxaria preserve little of his story beyond his faithful confession and his death for the Lord, in company with other martyrs of that city.

    Name days: Hippolytos, Hippolyte

  • Three Holy Hierarchs

    Great Feast, Τρεις Ιεράρχαι

    The feast of the Three Holy Hierarchs honors together Sts. Basil the Great, Gregory the Theologian, and John Chrysostom, the great fathers and ecumenical teachers of the Church. According to tradition, the common feast was established in the eleventh century after disputes arose in Constantinople over which of the three was greatest; the hierarchs made known in a vision that they are one before God, and a shared commemoration was appointed.

    Name days: Basil, Gregory, John, Vasilis, Grigoris, Giannis

  • Chryse the Martyr

    Holy Woman, Χρυσή

    St. Chryse was a Christian maiden of Rome in the third century who, tradition relates, had been exiled for her faith and lived at Ostia, where she encouraged the confessors imprisoned there. She was tortured and drowned in the sea with a stone about her neck.

    Name days: Chryse, Chrysi, Chrysa, Chrysoula

  • Kensourinos and Sabinos

    Martyr, Κενσουρίνος και Σαβαΐνος

    Sts. Kensourinos, a Roman magistrate, and Sabinos were among those who confessed Christ at Ostia in the third century together with St. Hippolytos and St. Chryse. Tradition relates that Kensourinos believed after seeing a dead man raised through the prayers of a Christian priest, and was beheaded.

  • Theophilos the New

    Martyr, Θεόφιλος ο Νέος

    St. Theophilos the New was a commander of the Byzantine forces in Cyprus in the eighth century. Captured by the Arabs, he was held for years in prison and, refusing to deny Christ, was beheaded around 792.

  • Theodore Hatzis of Mytilene

    Martyr, Θεόδωρος ο Χατζής

    St. Theodore was a Christian of Mytilene who had in a moment of weakness embraced Islam. Repenting, he returned openly to Christ, confessed his faith before the judge, and was hanged in Mytilene in 1784.

  • Finding of the Icon of the Annunciation in Tinos

    Saint, Ευαγγελίστρια Τήνου

    On January 30, 1823, after visions granted to the nun St. Pelagia of Tinos, the wonderworking icon of the Annunciation of the Theotokos was found buried in a field on the island of Tinos. The great church of the Evangelistria was built over the spot, and the island became the foremost place of pilgrimage in Greece.

  • Pelagia of Diveyevo

    Holy Woman, Πελαγία του Ντιβέεβο

    St. Pelagia was a married woman of Arzamas who, with the blessing of St. Seraphim of Sarov, took up the life of a fool-for-Christ and lived at the Diveyevo convent, enduring beatings and mockery with patience. She was granted the gifts of clairvoyance and healing.
