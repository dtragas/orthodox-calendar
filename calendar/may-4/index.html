Orthodox saints and name days on May 4

The Orthodox Church commemorates 7 saints and feasts on May 4. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on May 17 of the civil calendar.

4May

Name days on May 4

Pelagia, Pelayia, Pela, Nikiforos, Nikephoros, Nicephorus, Monica, Monika, Monique, Aphrodisios, Afrodisios, Leontios, Leontius, Florian, Ilarion, Hilarion

Saints and feasts of May 4

  • Pelagia

    Holy Woman, Πελαγία

    The Holy Martyr Pelagia was a young Christian woman of the early Church who suffered during the persecutions of the pagan emperors. Refusing to deny Christ or to be turned from her purity, she gave her life rather than betray her Lord, and the synaxaria honor her steadfast confession.

    Name days: Pelagia, Pelayia, Pela

  • Nikephoros of Mount Athos

    Venerable, Νικηφόρος ο Αθωνίτης

    Nikephoros was of Italian birth and, having embraced Orthodoxy, became a monk on Mount Athos in the thirteenth century. He lived in stillness in the wilder parts of the Mountain and wrote a treatise on watchfulness and the prayer of the heart.

    Name days: Nikiforos, Nikephoros, Nicephorus

  • Monica, Mother of Augustine

    Holy Woman, Μόνικα

    Monica was born in North Africa in the fourth century to a Christian family and was given in marriage to a pagan husband, whom she eventually won to the faith by patience. She wept and prayed for many years for her gifted but wayward son Augustine.

    Name days: Monica, Monika, Monique

  • Aphrodisios and Companions

    Martyr, Αφροδίσιος, Λεόντιος και Αντωνίνος

    Aphrodisios, together with Leontios, Antoninos, Valerian, Makrobios, Misdas and others, confessed Christ at Scythopolis in Palestine. Refusing every inducement to sacrifice, they were tortured and put to death.

    Name days: Aphrodisios, Afrodisios, Leontios, Leontius

  • Athanasios of Corinth

    Hierarch, Αθανάσιος Κορίνθου

    Athanasios was a monk who was raised to the see of Corinth, traditionally placed in the tenth century. He governed the church of Corinth with gentleness and kept his monastic discipline as a bishop.

  • Florian the Martyr

    Martyr, Φλωριανός

    Florian was an officer of the Roman army in the province of Noricum. During the persecution of Diocletian he openly declared himself a Christian when the governor sought out believers, and after scourging he was thrown into the river Enns with a stone tied to his neck, around the year 304.

    Name days: Florian

  • Hilarion the Wonderworker

    Venerable, Ιλαρίων ο Θαυματουργός

    The synaxarion records that Venerable Hilarion lived a life of asceticism and was granted the gift of working wonders, reposing in peace. Beyond this little has been preserved.

    Name days: Ilarion, Hilarion
