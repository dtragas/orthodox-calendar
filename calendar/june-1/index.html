Orthodox saints and name days on June 1

The Orthodox Church commemorates 7 saints and feasts on June 1. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on June 14 of the civil calendar.

1June

Name days on June 1

Ioustinos, Justin, Chariton, Hariton, Charito, Charitini, Thespesios, Thespesius, Agapitos, Agapitus

Saints and feasts of June 1

  • Justin the Philosopher

    Martyr, Ιουστίνος

    St. Justin the Philosopher and Martyr was born in Palestine and lived in the second century. Seeking truth through the schools of the philosophers, he found it at last in Christ, and thereafter wore the philosopher's cloak in the service of the Gospel. In Rome he wrote his celebrated Apologies, defending the Christians before the emperors and describing the baptism and Eucharist of the early Church.

    Name days: Ioustinos, Justin

  • Chariton, Charito and Companions, Martyrs with Justin

    Martyr, Χαρίτων και Χαριτώ

    The companions of Saint Justin the Philosopher were arrested with him in Rome under the prefect Rusticus during the reign of Marcus Aurelius. Among them was the virgin Charito and the layman Chariton, together with Evelpistus, Hierax, Paeon, Liberian and a second Justus.

    Name days: Chariton, Hariton, Charito, Charitini

  • Thespesios the Martyr

    Martyr, Θεσπέσιος

    Saint Thespesios was a Christian of Cappadocia who confessed Christ before the governor during the reign of Alexander Severus in the third century. Tradition relates that he was subjected to various tortures and finally beheaded, receiving the crown of martyrdom.

    Name days: Thespesios, Thespesius

  • Justin Popovich of Chelije

    Venerable, Ιουστίνος Πόποβιτς

    Saint Justin Popovich (1894-1979) was a Serbian priest-monk, professor of dogmatic theology and one of the great Orthodox theologians of the twentieth century. Removed from the university by the communist authorities, he spent his last decades confined to the women's monastery of Chelije near Valjevo, where he wrote his multi-volume Lives of the Saints and many theological works.

  • Dionysius of Glushitsa

    Venerable, Διονύσιος του Γλουσέτσκ

    Saint Dionysius of Glushitsa was a fifteenth-century Russian monastic who founded several monasteries in the forests of Vologda, in the region known as the Northern Thebaid. He was renowned as a strict ascetic, a painter of icons and a father to many disciples, and he reposed in 1437.

  • Agapitus of the Kiev Caves

    Venerable, Αγαπητός ο Ανάργυρος

    Saint Agapitus was a monk of the Kiev Caves Lavra in the eleventh century, a disciple of Saint Anthony of Kiev. He tended the sick of the monastery and of the city with prayer and humble remedies, asking no payment, and so received the title of Unmercenary Physician.

    Name days: Agapitos, Agapitus

  • Pyrrhus the Virgin, Bishop

    Hierarch, Πύρρος

    Saint Pyrrhus is remembered in the synaxaria as a bishop who preserved virginity throughout his life and shepherded his flock in holiness. Little else of his life has been preserved, and he is honoured simply as a saintly hierarch who reposed in peace.
