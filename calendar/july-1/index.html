Orthodox saints and name days on July 1

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on July 1. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on July 14 of the civil calendar.

Name days on July 1

Saints and feasts of July 1

  • Kosmas and Damian of Rome

    Martyr, Κοσμάς και Δαμιανός

    Sts. Kosmas and Damian of Rome were brothers and physicians of the third century who healed the sick freely in the name of Christ, accepting no payment, for which they are counted among the Holy Unmercenaries. Their charity and their faith drew many to the Church, and according to tradition they ended their lives as martyrs.

    Name days: Kosmas, Cosmas, Damian, Damianos

  • Peter the Patrician of Constantinople

    Venerable, Πέτρος ο Πατρίκιος

    St. Peter the Patrician was a nobleman and military officer at the court of Constantinople in the late eighth century. Tradition relates that after being taken captive in war and delivered from prison, he renounced his rank and wealth and entered the monastic life.

  • Leon the Hermit

    Venerable, Λέων ο Ερημίτης

    St. Leon the Hermit is remembered in the Synaxarion as an ascetic who withdrew from the world to live in solitude, prayer and fasting. Little is recorded of his life beyond his ascetic labors.

  • Mavrikios the Martyr

    Martyr, Μαυρίκιος

    St. Mavrikios (Maurice) is commemorated on this day as a martyr who confessed Christ before the pagan authorities and endured torments rather than deny his faith.

  • Konstantinos the Wonderworker and those with him

    Saint, Κωνσταντίνος ο Θαυματουργός

    St. Konstantinos the Wonderworker is venerated in Cyprus, where tradition relates that he came with a company of ascetics and confessed Christ. He and those with him suffered martyrdom for the faith.

  • Twenty-Five Martyrs of Nicomedia

    Martyr, Εικοσιπέντε Μάρτυρες Νικομηδείας

    The Twenty-Five Martyrs of Nicomedia were Christians who refused to sacrifice to idols during the persecutions of the early Church. Tradition relates that they were burned alive for their confession of Christ.
