Orthodox saints and name days on March 25

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on March 25. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on April 7 of the civil calendar.

Saints and feasts of March 25

  • Annunciation

    Great Feast, Ευαγγελισμός

    The Great Feast of the Annunciation commemorates the day the Archangel Gabriel was sent to the Virgin Mary in Nazareth to announce that she would conceive and bear the Son of God. Her humble acceptance as the handmaid of the Lord opened the way for the Incarnation, and at that moment the Word of God took flesh in her womb.

    Name days: Evangelos, Vangelis, Vaggelis, Vagelis, Evangelis, Evangelia, Lia, Vangelitsa, Evangelina, Vangelio, Vaggelio, Evangeline

  • Sennouphios the Sign-Bearer

    Venerable, Σεννούφιος

    St. Sennouphios was an ascetic of Egypt in the fourth century, famed for his miracles and called the Sign-Bearer. Tradition relates that the emperor Theodosius the Great, hearing of his holiness, asked for his prayers before a battle, and that Sennouphios sent him his worn cloak and staff, by which the emperor was victorious.

  • Pelagia and Theodosia

    Holy Woman, Πελαγία και Θεοδοσία

    Sts. Pelagia and Theodosia are martyrs whose names the Greek synaxaria record together for March 25, the feast of the Annunciation. Tradition relates that they confessed Christ and were put to death for the faith.

  • Demios the Martyr

    Martyr, Δήμιος

    St. Demios is a martyr whose name the Greek synaxaria record for March 25 with the note that he completed his witness by the sword. Nothing further of his life has been preserved.

  • Timon the Hermit

    Venerable, Τίμων ο Ερημίτης

    St. Timon was a monk of Russia in the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, a disciple of the elder Seraphim of Sarov according to tradition, who restored the hermitage of Nadeevo in the forests of Kostroma. He lived in great poverty and prayer, and reposed in 1840.

  • Parthenios of Kiev

    Venerable, Παρθένιος του Κιέβου

    St. Parthenios was a hieroschemamonk of the Kiev Caves Lavra in the nineteenth century, known for his unceasing prayer, his reading of the whole Psalter daily, and his gift of spiritual counsel. He reposed in 1855, on the feast of the Annunciation.
