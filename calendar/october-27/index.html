Orthodox saints and name days on October 27

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on October 27. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on November 9 of the civil calendar.

Saints and feasts of October 27

  • Nestor the Martyr

    Martyr, Νέστωρ

    St. Nestor was a young Christian of Thessaloniki, a disciple of the Great Martyr Demetrios. When the gladiator Lyaios was slaying challengers to the delight of the Emperor Maximian, Nestor received the blessing of the imprisoned Demetrios, entered the arena with the prayer, God of Demetrios, help me, and overthrew the boastful champion.

    Name days: Nestoras, Nestor

  • Prokla the Wife of Pilate

    Holy Woman, Πρόκλα

    The Gospel of Matthew records that Pilate's wife sent word to him during the trial of Christ, saying she had suffered much in a dream because of that righteous man and urging him to have nothing to do with Him.

  • Capitolina and Erotheis

    Holy Woman, Καπετωλίνα και Ερωτηΐς

    Capitolina was a wealthy noblewoman of Cappadocia who freed her slaves and gave her goods to the poor before presenting herself to the governor as a Christian. She was beheaded around 304.

  • Kyriakos of Constantinople

    Hierarch, Κυριάκος Πατριάρχης

    Kyriakos was steward of the Great Church of Constantinople and was elected Patriarch in 595, succeeding Saint John the Faster. He governed the Church in the reigns of Maurice and Phocas and built a church of the Theotokos in the Diakonissa quarter.

  • Demetrios the New of Basarabov

    Venerable, Δημήτριος ο Νέος

    Demetrios was a shepherd of the village of Basarabov on the river Lom in Bulgaria in the thirteenth century who became a monk and then a hermit in a cave near the village, living in prayer and great humility.

  • Nestor the Chronicler

    Venerable, Νέστωρ ο Χρονογράφος

    Nestor entered the Kiev Caves monastery in his youth under Saint Theodosios and was ordained hierodeacon. He wrote the lives of Saints Boris and Gleb and of Saint Theodosios, and is honored as the author of the Tale of Bygone Years, the earliest chronicle of the Rus.
