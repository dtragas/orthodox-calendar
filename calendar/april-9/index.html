Orthodox saints and name days on April 9

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on April 9. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on April 22 of the civil calendar.

Name days on April 9

Eudemos, Rafail, Raphael, Rafael, Rafaella, Rafaela, Raffaella, Efpsychios, Eupsychius, Vadim, Bademos, Bademus

Saints and feasts of April 9

  • Eudemos

    Martyr, Εύδημος

    St. Eudemos is remembered among the martyrs of the early Church, one who confessed Christ in the time of the persecutions and sealed his faith with suffering. The synaxaria preserve little of his life beyond this faithful witness.

    Name days: Eudemos

  • Raphael, Nicholas and Irene of Lesbos

    Martyr, Ραφαήλ, Νικόλαος και Ειρήνη

    Raphael, an archimandrite, and his deacon Nicholas were living at the monastery of Karyes near Thermi on Lesbos when the Turks attacked in 1463, ten years after the fall of Constantinople. With them was the twelve-year-old Irene, daughter of the village elder, and others who had taken refuge there.

    Name days: Rafail, Raphael, Rafael, Rafaella, Rafaela, Raffaella

  • Eupsychios of Caesarea

    Martyr, Ευψύχιος

    Eupsychios was a young Christian of Caesarea in Cappadocia, newly married, when the emperor Julian the Apostate sought to restore pagan worship. With other Christians he took part in the destruction of a pagan temple in the city.

    Name days: Efpsychios, Eupsychius

  • Bademus of Persia

    Martyr, Βάδιμος

    Bademus was born to a wealthy family in Persia and, giving away his possessions, founded a monastery near Bethlapat, gathering disciples around him. In the persecution of King Shapur he was imprisoned with seven of his monks.

    Name days: Vadim, Bademos, Bademus

  • Abdiesus and the Martyrs of Persia

    Saint, Αβδιησούς και οι συν αυτώ

    Abdiesus was among a great company of Christians, clergy and laity, taken captive in the Persian wars and held in Persia during the persecution of King Shapur. When they refused to worship the sun they were put to death together.

  • Acacius of Amida

    Hierarch, Ακάκιος Αμίδης

    Acacius was Bishop of Amida in Mesopotamia in the fifth century. When thousands of Persian prisoners of war were dying of hunger, he sold the gold and silver vessels of his church to feed them and send them home.
