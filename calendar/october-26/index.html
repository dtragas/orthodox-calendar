Orthodox saints and name days on October 26

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on October 26. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on November 8 of the civil calendar.

Saints and feasts of October 26

  • Demetrios the Great Martyr

    Martyr, Δημήτριος ο Μεγαλομάρτυς

    St. Demetrios the Great Martyr and Myrrh-Streamer was a young nobleman and military commander of Thessaloniki, martyred under the Emperor Maximian at the beginning of the fourth century. Imprisoned for openly teaching the faith, he blessed the young Nestor, who defeated the gladiator Lyaios in the arena; for this Demetrios was run through with spears in his prison.

    Name days: Dimitrios, Dimitris, Dimitri, Mimis, Takis, Demetrius, Demetrios, Jimmy, Dmitri, Dmitry, Mitsos, Jim, Demetris, Dimitra, Demetra, Dimitroula, Mimi, Demi

  • Commemoration of the Great Earthquake

    Saint, Μνήμη Μεγάλου Σεισμού

    On October 26, 740, in the reign of the iconoclast emperor Leo the Isaurian, a great earthquake struck Constantinople, destroying walls and churches. The people understood it as a call to repentance and turned to the Theotokos and Saint Demetrios, whose feast it was.

  • Loupos the Martyr

    Martyr, Λούππος

    Loupos was the faithful servant of Saint Demetrios who stood by his master at his martyrdom, gathered his blood-stained garment and ring, and worked healings with them among the people of Thessalonica.

  • Joasaph of Dionysiou

    Martyr, Ιωάσαφ ο Οσιομάρτυς

    Joasaph was a monk and iconographer of the monastery of Dionysiou on Mount Athos, tonsured by the abbot Stephen. Longing for martyrdom, he went to Constantinople with the abbot's blessing.

  • Artemidoros and Basil

    Martyr, Αρτεμίδωρος και Βασίλειος

    Artemidoros and Basil are commemorated together in the Synaxarion of this day. Tradition relates that they confessed Christ and were beheaded; the details of their lives are not preserved.

  • George of Ioannina (Translation of Relics)

    Saint, Γεώργιος ο εν Ιωαννίνοις

    George, a poor groom from the village of Tzourchli in Epirus, was hanged at Ioannina in 1838 for refusing to deny Christ after a false accusation that he had once become a Muslim. His relics were found incorrupt and became a source of many miracles.
