Orthodox saints and name days on August 25

The Orthodox Church commemorates 7 saints and feasts on August 25. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on September 7 of the civil calendar.

25August

Name days on August 25

Bartholomeos, Bartholomew, Bartolo, Chrysostomos, Chrysostom, Titos, Titus

Saints and feasts of August 25

  • Return of the Relics of Bartholomew

    Apostle, Βαρθολομαίος

    This feast commemorates the translation of the relics of the Holy Apostle Bartholomew, one of the Twelve, who preached the Gospel in eastern lands and completed his apostolic course as a martyr. According to tradition, his relics, placed in a coffin and cast into the sea by persecutors, were carried by the waves and received with honor by the faithful, coming at last to the island of Lipari.

    Name days: Bartholomeos, Bartholomew, Bartolo

  • Chrysostomos the New of Florina

    Hierarch, Χρυσόστομος ο Νέος Φλωρίνης

    St. Chrysostomos the New, Metropolitan of Florina, was a hierarch and confessor of the twentieth century. A learned and ascetic bishop, he took up the leadership of the Genuine Orthodox Christians of Greece who held to the traditional church calendar, and for this confession he endured deprivation, censure, and exile, bearing all with patience and meekness.

    Name days: Chrysostomos, Chrysostom

  • Titus the Apostle

    Apostle, Τίτος

    Saint Titus was a native of Crete and a disciple and companion of the Apostle Paul, who addressed to him one of the Pastoral Epistles. Paul left him in Crete to organize the Church there, and he became its first bishop.

    Name days: Titos, Titus

  • John the Karpathian

    Venerable, Ιωάννης ο Καρπάθιος

    Saint John came from the island of Karpathos and lived the monastic life, leaving writings of counsel to monks that were later included in the Philokalia.

  • Epiphanios and John, Patriarchs of Constantinople

    Hierarch, Επιφάνιος και Ιωάννης Κωνσταντινουπόλεως

    Saint John the Cappadocian and his successor Saint Epiphanios governed the Church of Constantinople in the early sixth century, in the reigns of Justin and Justinian. Both laboured to restore communion with Rome after the Acacian schism.

  • Menas Patriarch of Constantinople

    Hierarch, Μηνάς Κωνσταντινουπόλεως

    Saint Menas was Patriarch of Constantinople in the reign of Justinian the Great, in whose time the Church of Hagia Sophia was built. He presided over the Church in the years before the Fifth Ecumenical Council.

  • Konstantia of Paphos

    Holy Woman, Κωνσταντία η εν Πάφω

    Saint Konstantia lived in Paphos in Cyprus and was a devoted disciple of Saint Hilarion the Great, whose relics she guarded after his repose.
