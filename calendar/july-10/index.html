Orthodox saints and name days on July 10

The Orthodox Church commemorates 8 saints and feasts on July 10. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on July 23 of the civil calendar.

Saints and feasts of July 10

  • Anthony of the Kiev Caves

    Venerable, Αντώνιος

    St. Anthony of the Kiev Caves is honored as the father of monasticism in the lands of Rus. Born at Lyubech, he received the monastic tonsure on Mount Athos, and returning north he settled in a cave above the Dnieper River near Kiev, where his holiness drew disciples and the great Monastery of the Caves took root.

  • Forty-Five Holy Martyrs of Nicopolis

    Martyr, Μάρτυρες

    The Forty-Five Holy Martyrs of Nicopolis in Armenia confessed Christ during the persecution of the Emperor Licinius, around the year 319. Presenting themselves openly as Christians, they were imprisoned and endured harsh sufferings, encouraging one another in prayer and psalmody, and were finally put to death by fire.

  • Amalia

    Holy Woman, Αμαλία

    In the Greek Orthodox tradition, those who bear the name Amalia keep their name day on July 10, together with the commemoration of the Forty-Five Holy Martyrs of Nicopolis. The name, of old Germanic origin, carries the sense of industriousness and care, and came into Greek usage in more recent centuries.

    Name days: Amalia, Amalie, Amelie, Amelia, Amalberga

  • Bianor and Silouanos

    Martyr, Βιάνωρ και Σιλουανός

    St. Bianor was a Christian of Pisidia who was tortured for refusing to sacrifice to idols. Tradition relates that Silouanos, seeing his courage, confessed Christ as well.

  • Apollonios of Sardis

    Martyr, Απολλώνιος Σάρδεων

    St. Apollonios was a Christian of Sardis in Lydia who refused to swear by the fortune of the emperor. Brought before the governor at Iconium, he confessed Christ openly.

  • Ten Thousand Fathers of Nitria

    Martyr, Μύριοι Πατέρες

    The Ten Thousand Fathers were monks of the Egyptian desert who were slain in the time of Patriarch Theophilos of Alexandria, who had them accused of Origenism. Tradition relates that they were killed by fire and sword in their monasteries.

  • Parthenios and Eumenios of Koudoumas

    Venerable, Παρθένιος και Ευμένιος

    Sts. Parthenios and Eumenios were brothers from Pigaidakia in Crete who lived as ascetics and refounded the Monastery of Koudoumas on the southern coast of the island. They lived in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

  • Synaxis of the Saints of Vatopedi

    Saint, Σύναξις Βατοπαιδινών Αγίων

    On this day the Monastery of Vatopedi on Mount Athos commemorates together all the saints who lived and labored within its walls: founders, ascetics, hierarchs and martyrs.
