Orthodox saints and name days on February 14

The Orthodox Church commemorates 7 saints and feasts on February 14. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on February 27 of the civil calendar.

14February

Name days on February 14

Saints and feasts of February 14

  • Auxentios

    Venerable, Αυξέντιος

    St. Auxentios lived in the fifth century and served for a time at the imperial court in Constantinople before forsaking the world for the life of a hermit. He settled on a mountain in Bithynia, not far from Chalcedon, where he lived in a small cell in fasting and prayer, receiving the many who climbed to him for counsel and healing.

    Name days: Auxentios

  • Maron the Hermit

    Venerable, Μάρων

    St. Maron lived in the open air on a mountain near Cyrrhus in Syria, where he turned a former pagan temple into a place of prayer. Theodoret records that he received the gift of healing and delivered many from illness and demons.

  • Valentine the Hieromartyr

    Hierarch, Βαλεντίνος

    Two martyrs named Valentine are commemorated on this day: a presbyter of Rome and the Bishop of Terni in Italy, both of whom suffered in the third century. Tradition relates that they healed the sick and brought many to Christ, for which they were arrested and beheaded.

    Name days: Valentinos, Valentine, Valentin, Valentina, Valentini

  • George Paizanos of Mytilene

    Martyr, Γεώργιος ο Παϊζάνος

    St. George, called Paizanos, was born on the island of Lesvos and worked as a tailor. Tradition relates that he was denounced to the Turkish authorities in Constantinople and pressed to change his faith.

  • Nicholas of Psari

    Martyr, Νικόλαος ο εξ Ιχθύος

    St. Nicholas was born in the village of Psari in Corinthia and, orphaned young, went to Selymbria where he married, and later worked as a grocer in Constantinople. Falsely accused of having accepted Islam, he was imprisoned and tortured.

  • Nicholas of Trebizond

    Hierarch, Νικόλαος ο Τραπεζούντιος

    St. Nicholas came from Trebizond and lived in the region of Amaseia in Pontus. From youth he wished to dedicate himself to God, and after the death of his wife and child he became a priest and served the faithful with zeal.

  • Philemon of Gaza

    Hierarch, Φιλήμων Γάζης

    St. Philemon, Bishop of Gaza, is recorded in an early Patmian manuscript as a hieromartyr of the Church. No further details of his life have been preserved.
