Orthodox saints and name days on July 26

The Orthodox Church commemorates 6 saints and feasts on July 26. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep this day on August 8 of the civil calendar.

Saints and feasts of July 26

  • Paraskevi the Great Martyr

    Martyr, Παρασκευή η Μεγαλομάρτυς

    St. Paraskevi was born near Rome in the second century to devout Christian parents, who named her for the day of the Passion of the Lord, Friday, in Greek Paraskevi. After their death she gave her goods to the poor and went about preaching Christ, for which she was arrested; she endured torture with steadfastness and was finally beheaded, her witness, according to tradition, bringing even her persecutors to wonder.

    Name days: Paraskeva, Paraskevas, Paraskevi, Paraskeve, Voula, Vouli, Vassoula, Evi, Vivi, Skevi

  • Hermolaos, Hermippos and Hermokrates

    Hierarch, Ερμόλαος, Έρμιππος και Ερμοκράτης

    St. Hermolaos was a priest of Nicomedia who survived the burning of the twenty thousand martyrs and lived in hiding, where he instructed the young Panteleimon in the faith and baptized him. Denounced during the trial of Panteleimon, he confessed Christ together with Hermippos and Hermokrates.

    Name days: Ermolaos, Hermolaos, Hermolaus, Ermolaus

  • Oraiozele the Virgin Martyr

    Holy Woman, Ωραιοζήλη

    St. Oraiozele was, according to tradition, a disciple of the Apostle Andrew who confessed Christ in the region of Byzantium. She was tortured and beheaded for her faith.

    Name days: Oraiozeli, Oreozili, Oraiozele, Oreozele

  • Ierousalem the Martyr

    Holy Woman, Ιερουσαλήμ

    St. Ierousalem was a Christian woman of Berroia in Macedonia who, tradition relates, was martyred together with her children for her confession of Christ.

  • Gerontios of Athos

    Venerable, Γερόντιος ο Αγιορείτης

    St. Gerontios was an ascetic of Mount Athos who is honored as the founder of the Skete of St. Anne, the oldest and largest of the Athonite sketes. Tradition relates that he lived in the fourteenth century.

  • Jacob Netsvetov of Alaska

    Venerable, Ιάκωβος ο εν Αλάσκα

    St. Jacob Netsvetov was born in Alaska to a Russian father and an Aleut mother and was the first native Alaskan ordained to the priesthood. For decades he traveled the Aleutian Islands and the Yukon, translating the Scriptures and baptizing thousands.
