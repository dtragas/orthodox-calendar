Constantine and Helen

Κωνσταντίνος και Ελένη

Great Feast

Feast day May 21

Equal-to-the-Apostles Emperor Constantine the Great and his mother Helen.

St. Constantine the Great, Equal-to-the-Apostles, was the first Roman Emperor to embrace Christianity. After his vision of the Cross and his victory under its sign, he ended the persecutions with the Edict of Milan, convened the First Ecumenical Council at Nicaea, and founded Constantinople as a Christian capital. His mother, St. Helen, traveled in old age to the Holy Land, where according to tradition she found the Precious Cross of the Lord and built churches on the holy sites.

Mother and son are commemorated together on May 21, one of the great name days of the Greek world, honored as Equals-to-the-Apostles for turning an empire toward Christ.

Feast day

On the New Calendar, Constantine and Helen is commemorated on May 21. Churches on the Old (Julian) Calendar keep the feast on June 3 of the civil calendar.

Who celebrates a name day

People with these names keep their name day on this feast:

Konstantinos, Kostas, Kostis, Dinos, Konstandinos, Constantine, Costas, Costa, Gus, Konstantin, Eleni, Elena, Helen, Elli, Lena, Heleni, Konstantina, Constantina, Dina, Ntina, Kostoula, Helena, Ellen, Elenitsa, Nitsa, Lenio

    Venerable, Κασσιανός ο Έλληνας

    Venerable Kassianos the Greek, wonderworker of Uglich.

  • Constantine of Murom with his sons Michael and Theodore

    Saint, Κωνσταντίνος του Μουρόμ

    Holy Princes Constantine, Michael and Theodore of Murom, enlighteners of that region.

  • Christopher of Antioch

    Hierarch, Χριστόφορος Αντιοχείας

    Hieromartyr Christopher I, Patriarch of Antioch, murdered for his faith.

  • Timothy, Polios and Eutychios

    Martyr, Τιμόθεος, Πόλιος και Ευτύχιος

    Holy Martyrs Timothy, Polios and Eutychios, deacons of Africa.
