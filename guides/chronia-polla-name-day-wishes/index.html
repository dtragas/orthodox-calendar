Χρόνια Πολλά: What It Means and What to Say on a Name Day

Χρόνια Πολλά (Chronia Polla, roughly “HROH-nya poh-LAH”) is the most useful phrase in Greek social life. Literally “many years,” it is the all-purpose blessing for name days, birthdays, and great feasts — a compact prayer that God grant the person many more years, full and blessed.

What it means, and when to say it

The wish descends from the Byzantine acclamation of “many years” sung for emperors and patriarchs, and it survives in the Church's Polychronion — the “Many Years” chanted for the living at celebrations. In everyday life you say Χρόνια Πολλά:

  • On someone's name day — the classic occasion (see what a name day is)
  • On birthdays
  • On great feasts — Christmas, Theophany, Dormition — to everyone you meet
  • On Pascha, though there the proper exchange is Χριστός Ἀνέστη! (“Christ is Risen!”) answered by Ἀληθῶς Ἀνέστη! (“Truly He is Risen!”)

Its cousins refine the wish: Χρόνια Πολλά και καλά (“many good years”), Να τα εκατοστίσεις (“may you reach a hundred”), and for the name day specifically, Και του χρόνου — “and again next year.”

Name-day etiquette: the call matters

In Greek and much of Orthodox culture, the burden of remembering falls on the well-wisher. The celebrant doesn't send invitations; friends are simply expected to remember and reach out — a call is warmest, a message is welcome, and showing up (in villages, unannounced) is best of all. Forgetting a close friend's name day genuinely stings, which is why the question “whose name day is it today?” is a daily reflex in Orthodox households — and why the person who always remembers first earns a special kind of affection.

Wishes you can send today

Short and warm beats long and formal. Some ready examples:

Χρόνια Πολλά! May your saint always watch over you and your family. Many years!
Happy name day! Wishing you health, joy, and God's blessings — Χρόνια Πολλά και καλά!
Χρόνια Πολλά for your feast! May you celebrate it for a hundred years — και του χρόνου!
Many years! May the prayers of your patron saint keep you strong all year long.

For someone named after the saint of a Great Feast — a Maria on the Dormition, a Christos at Christmas — add a word for the feast itself: “Blessed feast, and Χρόνια Πολλά for your name day!” (See the guide to feast days.)

The hard part is remembering — the wishing is easy

Nobody forgets what to say; people forget the day. With name days scattered across the calendar — and doubled if your family spans the Old and New calendars — the well-wisher's duty needs a little help.
